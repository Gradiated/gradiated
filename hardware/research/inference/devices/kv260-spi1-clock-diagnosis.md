# KV260 SPI1 clock diagnosis

On 17 November 2026, we reproduced the SPI1 wake on the idle KV260 with the original 50 MHz circuit loaded, on the same boot as Experiment 10. Linux exposed one child on SPI1 (`ff050000.spi`), the Infineon SLB9670 TPM at `spi2.0`. Linux selected this TPM as its hardware random-number source, and the kernel's RNG worker was present in both process snapshots. [K26 device tree](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/v6.8/arch/arm64/boot/dts/xilinx/zynqmp-sm-k26-revA.dts) · [Linux hardware RNG worker](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/v6.8/drivers/char/hw_random/core.c)

During 72 seconds of passive sampling, SPI1 woke alongside 106 TPM transfers (387 bytes) and subsequently went back to sleep. The live autosuspend delay was 3 seconds. SPI1 transfer counts stayed flat during separate clock reads and a state snapshot. The boot-firmware status command made 12 transfers (416 bytes) on the separate QSPI flash controller, while SPI1 stayed suspended. The original firmware, overlays, FPGA manager and 50 MHz clock checks passed before and after the test. [Cadence SPI driver](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/v6.8/drivers/spi/spi-cadence.c)

Background TPM activity is the strongest explanation for the reproduced wake. The third candidate's after-arm clock read came more than 13 seconds after its energy window ended. The experiment's endpoint snapshots cannot establish whether TPM activity overlapped any measured interval or how much energy it contributed.
