# Experiment 13 · Run two exponentials within one attention head

Aidan authorised one comparison at 50 MHz on the KV260. Compare the adopted attention-exp-div-v1 against the same design with two copies of its exact exponential arithmetic. Keep the frozen model, all fixed-point rules, head order, cache traffic, scalar probability divider, host and workload fixed. Each head processes adjacent scores in pairs and uses one unit for the odd tail. The hypothesis is that overlapping the two exponential calculations saves attention wait clocks, provided both units and their tables route at 50 MHz.

Read two scores on successive clocks through the existing read port. Dispatch both commands together. Each unit reads two adjacent words from its private copy of the same writable exponential table. Require both units to accept each table write. Retire one result per clock, in ascending position order, to the original exponential buffer and running sum. A subsequent probability pass sees the same entire ordered sum. Keep independent division and context-mixing work unchanged.

The adopted full exponential uses 49 wait clocks, including the result handshake. The direct cutoff uses one. Serial service for a pair costs two reads, two launches and both waits. Overlapped service costs two reads, one launch, and the later of the first wait plus one or the second wait alone. Each full/full pair therefore saves 49 clocks. Single tails preserve the old schedule. Mixed pairs and fault paths are checked separately.
