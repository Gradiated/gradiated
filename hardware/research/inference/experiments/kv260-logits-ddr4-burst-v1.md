# Experiment 16 · Feed the eight final-score lanes from DDR4

How much on-chip memory can we recover by moving the final token-score weights to DDR4, and how long do the eight arithmetic lanes wait for them? [Experiment 15](../results/output-projection-ddr4-burst-20261119T111700Z-e69dad/findings.md) measured a smaller matrix and retained its on-chip copy. This comparison removes the final-score copy in the candidate. The table used for input embeddings stays on-chip. The two tables contain the same learned weights.

## Design and hypothesis

A is the selected [Experiment 13 circuit](../results/attention-exp-pair-v2-20261117T232821Z-e72da4/findings.md). B is logits-ddr4-burst-v1. Both run the frozen W16A16 model at 50 MHz, score 320 possible tokens in groups of eight and accumulate each dot product in its original order. Use the frozen three-prompt workload, the same host measurement boundaries and the same output acceptance rules.

The matrix has 320 rows of 32 signed 16-bit weights, 20,480 bytes in total. Pack the DDR copy by group, then by coordinate, then by lane so each pair of 64-bit responses provides one weight for each of eight lanes. For each group, stage 512 bytes locally in eight 64-byte bursts, then compute. Begin the next group's reads after the current group retires. This simple schedule exposes the cost of fetching weights separately from any future overlap experiment.

The hypothesis is that whole-design block RAM use will fall, and that decode will take longer as the lanes wait between groups. For each complete scoring pass expect 40 groups, 320 accepted read addresses, 2,560 accepted response beats and 20,480 payload bytes at HP0. Test the schedule prediction in simulation, then reconcile measured extra FPGA clocks with the number of scored positions, address stalls, response waits and staging overhead. Count prompt-only positions that skip final scores separately. Report the difference in actual mapped BRAM across both whole designs and within the logits hierarchy, along with buffer resources, DMA interface costs, and retained embedding storage where identifiable. The bytes in the table and the routed tiles saved measure different things.

## Qualification and bounds

Verify address order, signed lane mapping and tied weight identity independently against the frozen input. Check directed arithmetic cases, boundaries including the final group and final valid burst, backpressure, error, reset and safe draining. Replay both arms against frozen full-model cases, exact scores and completion behaviour. Allow 25 minutes for a full replay. A successful local unit test alone cannot qualify the board image. Reuse the exact prior control build once source, shell, constraints, image and reports match. For the candidate allow 42 minutes for a route, three minutes for archiving and five more for recovery of its artifacts. Require setup and hold closure, the expected resource hierarchy, the read-only HP0 route, verified artifact identities and an independently reviewed physical packet.

On the KV260 admit the verified original state, pin its boot and confirm that the previous driver's boot marker matches the recorded restoration. Use the temporary driver with its private aligned 20,480-byte contiguous buffer. Transfer ownership to the device through the Linux streaming DMA API before enabling FPGA reads. Keep the allocation alive until all outstanding reads have drained. Use a single board owner. Keep control-side model upload, candidate-specific packing, device setup, reconfiguration and readback checks outside timed requests and report their boundaries.

## Physical workload and measurements

Use four logical sessions A1 B1 B2 A2 and three loads A, B, A. For each smoke block or powered block, open a fresh native runner and load the same model outside the timing and power windows. Keep the weights resident through all replies within the block. The four sessions determine the ordering of blocks and image loads. After each load verify one smoke reply for each of the three prompts. Use the same five-second request limit for both arms. Smoke replies must each finish within five-thirds of a second. Plan 30 minutes for the run and reserve a separate three minutes for restoration.

Each session contains two mixed blocks, each with three warm-ups and six timed replies, two per prompt. Follow these with a separate block for each prompt, with one warm-up and 18 timed Hello, 12 six-byte or eight 48-byte replies. Pair mixed rounds 1 and 2 and the Hello, six-byte, 48-byte order in A1/B1. Pair rounds 3 and 4 and the reversed prompt order in B2/A2. The totals are nine smoke replies, 36 warm-ups, 200 timed replies and 20 powered blocks. Treat the two paired sessions as comparisons within one board boot.

Follow the [measurement standard](measurement-standard-v2.md). Record exact output/termination for every request, TTFT, decode tokens/s, completion time, stage and request clocks, transfer and wait counts where observable, spread, power, temperatures and resource counts. Preserve mixed and per-prompt gross VCC_SOM joules per answer and output token, tokens/J, idle/active watts, sample alignment sensitivity and SPI1/TPM brackets. These readings cover the module supply. They cannot isolate the energy of the DDR chips. Report missing observations explicitly. Stop on incorrect results, transport faults, counter mismatches or unsafe release. Verify restoration of the original research image, weights, HP0 state and all 320 BOS scores, and leave the board running. Preserve raw evidence on the Mac with its hashes and record verified outcomes separately for simulation, routing and physical measurement. Baseline adoption is a separate decision.
