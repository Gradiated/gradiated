# Open-loop request load on the KV260

**Status.** Schedule, bounded queue and analysis prepared. Execution requires separate authorisation and a reviewed board packet.

Use `scalar-div-v1` on one KV260 at 50 MHz with the frozen `chip-joke-text-20261109` W16A16 model and exact historical three-prompt references pinned in the [manifest](../workloads/kv260-request-load-v1.json). Use the same resident output cap and exact output, stop and context checks throughout. The question is how often one engine can deliver a verified answer within these response targets before queueing degrades service. Closed-loop speed and maximum isolated decode speed answer different questions.

## Arrival and outcome contract

An independent board-local producer offers requests at fixed offsets in a 60-second window. The consumer executes one request at a time on one FPGA context, using the same monotonic clock for offers, native delivery, completion and verification. No network client is involved. The FIFO has four waiting slots plus the single in-flight request.

Test periodic offers of 1, 3 and 5 requests/s, yielding 60, 180 and 300 scheduled requests per window. Also test four simultaneous offers every 4/3 seconds, yielding 180 requests at the same average 3 requests/s. The predeclared manifest rotates the order through three repetitions, for twelve windows and 2,160 planned requests overall. Each window balances the three prompt classes, rotates the first one and starts after one verified warm-up per case. The producer offers each burst under the same queue lock.

Record planned, offered and admitted times, waiting length at offer, service start, output timestamps, done, verification and terminal reason. Actual submission and offer are the same event at this board-local boundary. Reject offers at a full queue. A request admitted but still waiting 2 seconds after its planned arrival expires in the queue. Stop on a service, verification or native deadline failure and preserve the first failure. Pending requests are accounted for as expired or aborted, and future planned requests are marked unoffered. Report count reconciliation and queue history. Each service has a 3-second absolute limit from dispatch, bounded by the window watchdog. Stop admission at 60 seconds, allow up to 10 seconds to drain, then stop this window.

The provisional research response target is first useful byte within **400 ms** and exact verified completion within **1.5 seconds** of planned arrival. Both must pass for an SLO success, including first byte, so an empty output cannot meet these targets. Queue-full offers, expiries, failures and aborted requests count in the offered denominator. Missed offers or a maximum actual offer slip above 50 ms make the window invalid for capacity. Keep partial evidence and raw counts. These thresholds characterise the test rather than product availability.

## Reporting and admission

Report response times from planned and actual offer, wait from actual offer to service, service-only first byte and verified completion, plus per-request decode TPS and token gaps. Report pooled decode using total inter-token intervals divided by their total time. Report completed requests/s and useful output tokens/s over the observation interval including drain, and SLO-qualified completions per offered-window second. Keep the waiting backlog at the end of offers separate from the number still active and the total unfinished. Print one summary per workload and repetition, with per-class counts and results. One useful token is one output byte for this model.

Report medians and ranges. A pooled p95 may be reported descriptively using nearest rank and at least 100 verified samples for that rate and workload. Point out when it pools dependent repetitions. Keep individual windows and case mixes visible. Skip p99. To report the highest passing tested periodic rate, require all three valid repetitions to reach 95% SLO success among offered requests, no more than one queued request at the end of offers, and no more than one additional waiting slot on average in the final quarter versus the first quarter as sampled at offers. Report burst results separately. These are coarse queue-growth screens and finite-workload observations. There is one executing hardware context.

The worker callback is prepared against the existing `DeliveryBenchmark`. Pass its resident `runner.now` to the producer, dispatcher and service receipts and verify that native output timestamps share that clock. Before execution, pin the fresh off-board closure, reference artifact, UID/ownership and watchdog, and check matching clock domains and producer overhead using a separately reviewed adapter qualification. Reserve at most 90 seconds per window and 22 minutes including warm-ups under an outer experiment watchdog, with setup, restoration and evidence export separately bounded in the eventual packet. If collecting power, scope it to the VCC_SOM supply and the full declared observation interval including rejected/failed work and drain, with matched idle windows and raw coverage checks. The 77-second sampler envelope reserves both idle windows, guards and the longest drain. A scoped energy result requires the longer sampler/validator integration from the [handoff](kv260-milestone-preparation.md). Follow [measurement standard v2](measurement-standard-v2.md).
