## Candidate and forecast

The candidate, `ff-scalar-mul-dsp-v1`, replaces the multiplier in one feed-forward scalar unit. Each 64-bit operand has four 16-bit pieces. Compute all 16 partial products together and add the shifted results in stages, taking five calculation clocks in total. The earlier multiplier takes 16. Keep the feed-forward matrix multipliers, both attention scalars, normalisation and all other stages as in Experiment 18. Check the routed DSP placement and count, since the tool can simplify unused logic.

In the [earlier feed-forward trace](../results/ff-profile-rtl-20261116T211748Z/findings.md), the 128 elements each used two direct products and one product for exponential interpolation on all three natural prompts. If all those waits remain exposed on Experiment 18, the saving is 384 × 11 = 4,224 clocks per position. This projects 426,624 clocks (8.71%) saved on Hello, 570,240 (8.11%) on the six-byte prompt and 849,024 (7.23%) on the 48-byte prompt. Full-model simulation tests both the call count and how much delay disappears at the request boundaries.
