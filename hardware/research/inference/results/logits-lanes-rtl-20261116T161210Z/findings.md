# Final token-score lanes in matched RTL simulation

The one-lane control and both banked variants passed all seven fixed model cases. They each matched 103,360 retained scores, the valid cache contents, generated bytes, stop reasons and output limits. The two and four-lane components also passed independent signed product and accumulation checks for all 10,240 terms, ordered score stalls and reset at every active coordinate and stream lane.

| Prompt | One lane | Two lanes | Four lanes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Hello | 8,665,425 cycles | 8,170,385 cycles (−5.71%) | 7,922,865 cycles (−8.57%) |
| Six-byte prompt | 12,782,317 cycles | 12,113,197 cycles (−5.23%) | 11,778,637 cycles (−7.85%) |
| 48-byte prompt | 21,481,987 cycles | 20,910,787 cycles (−2.66%) | 20,625,187 cycles (−3.99%) |

Final projection time was 11,200, 5,760 and 3,040 cycles respectively. Compared with one lane, each scored position saved exactly 5,440 or 8,160 cycles. The prefix up to entry into the final prompt's score projection used the same number of cycles in all three arms. The differences are entirely in `LOGIT_WAIT`. Both new variants read one stored activation each cycle and broadcast it to all lanes. Their separate banks provide one weight per lane. The total logical weight storage and the order of emitted scores are unchanged.

Each projection consumes 20,480 bytes of weights. With one, two or four lanes, the banks supply 16, 32 or 64 weight bits on a read cycle alongside one shared 16-bit activation. Dividing the fixed weight count by the observed projection cycles at 50 MHz gives effective rates of **91.4, 177.8 and 336.8 MB/s**. These are derived rates across the complete projection, including priming, rounding and score delivery. They describe useful on-chip weight work in the simulation.
