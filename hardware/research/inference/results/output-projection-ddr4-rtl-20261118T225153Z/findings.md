# Exact DDR4 projection fixture replay

The two modes of one generated circuit matched all seven frozen model cases, covering 716 processed positions per mode. The checks covered every emitted logit and generated byte, stop reasons, 640 legacy BOS scores per case and mode, and byte-identical key/value cache at the prefill and end-of-request checkpoints. BRAM matched the adopted Experiment 13 phase and elapsed clocks exactly.

With the simulated AXI responder, each DDR projection accepted 1,024 read addresses and 1,024 eight-byte beats. It selected 2,048 useful bytes and added 4,608 FPGA clocks per projection. That was 2,048 fixed launch and address clocks, 1,024 address-backpressure clocks and 1,536 response-wait clocks. These increments accounted for every extra projection-phase and whole-request clock. They describe the simulation’s controlled wait schedule.

An independent arithmetic oracle checked every operand, AXI address, product, partial sum and output for one full projection in BRAM, DDR and perturbed DDR. It exercised positive and negative ties and saturation. Editing a single external coefficient changed row 6 from −50 to −52 with the BRAM coefficients held fixed. Accepted DDR commands in the model replay asserted nonsecure data access.

Injected SLVERR, a wrong response ID, missing RLAST, and address and response timeouts stopped inference with an error. AXI-Lite full reset while an address was stalled or a response was outstanding canceled calculation and kept safe release low through transport drain. Each case recovered through ordinary weight reload and checked all 320 BRAM BOS scores. A slave that never completes a transfer can hold safe release low indefinitely after inference has faulted.
