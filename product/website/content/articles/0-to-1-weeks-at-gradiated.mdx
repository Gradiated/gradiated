So what's going on around here?

## Post-training for PostHog AI

PostHog is one of our first customers. They have an agent that lets you chat about your data. It uses Anthropic's Sonnet 5.5 and it costs PostHog $100,000 in tokens to run each month. The spend is significant enough for them to wonder how they can save money without sacrificing performance.

To start, we sent Josh our co-founder as a Forward Deployed Engineer to investigate and benchmark. The idea is to guage how open-source models perform vs. Sonnet on recent data from production. The expectation is not that open-source models out of the box are able to match the Frontier model. More practically, we want to know how big is the difference in quality on this specific work, and how much can we close that gap?

Open-source models, by nature of being open-source, allow you to continue training the model. In pre-training, the model learns to perform well on a wide variety of tasks, and is subsequently post-trained to improve the model's behaviour and knowledge in more targetted areas. You can post-train a model to teach it how to call your specific tools, and you can also post-train to teach the model what good looks like on your specific work.

Here's how that's going.

In attempt to teach Qwen(link) what good responses to user requests looked like, we literally made performance worse than out-of-the-box Qwen. I was surprised by this but Josh was not. I didn't think post-training would guarantee an improvement, but I also didn't think degredation was such an easily accessible outcome. It's a bag of tricks approach, and this one didn't work. That's a bit too vague to be helpful, so here are the details.

How did we post-train?

> We did Supervised Fine Tuning (SFT), a common starting point. We built a dataset that started with ~5000 production traces, anonymized the data (this was hard, if you're curious let me know) and filtered that down to around 1000 conversations that would be challenging to the model.

Expectations?

> There is literature(link) suggesting using SFT in a situation like this can degrade model performance, but that's not a guarantee, so worth trying anyways. It indeed did degrade.

In a situation like what?

> The data used in post-training was pairs of what the user asked and what Sonnet responded with. SFT trains Qwen to answer like the Frontier model... but Qwen doesn't have the capabilites of the Frontier model.

Sonnet is confident, and it should be, it's great. But that level of confidence doesn't match Qwen's skills. Qwen may be able to solve the problem, but if it tries to solve the problem like Sonnet would it will do worse than if it just tried on its own.

That makes sense, how sure are we that this is what's happening?

> Medium confidence. It makes sense that this is what could be happening. It's a concern with "off-policy" training. "Off" because it's learning from another model and not itself. We could try to probe at why it's performing poorly, but that's a new experiment.


Josh is now working on "on-policy" post-training methods. "On" meaning Qwen can learn from it's own good and bad attempts at responding to a question. Results pending.


## FPGAs

Aidan is working on a long-term project. It's a knowledge building endeavour, and he is learning the fundamentals of how to build hardware. The approach is not what can we do with the hardware we have but what could we do if every part of the hardware chain was open for exploration.

He's giving the team weekly lectures, which feels like a special way to learn. This week he's working with an FPGA (field programmable gate array), a maximally customizable chip. Unlike a CPU or GPU, where the circuit is fixed and you write software for it, an FPGA lets you wire the circuit yourself. He's found a small language model that fits in the on-chip memory and the model tells jokes. We're curious how fast can it tell the jokes, how much heat that generates and what's the cost of doing so.

We've yet to see the jokes because finding a charger for the FPGA is reportedly much harder than programming it to serve the model. More on this when we can find one.
