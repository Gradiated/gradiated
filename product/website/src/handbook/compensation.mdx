## How it works

Pay scales at Gradiated run on a fair system, not on negotiation. It's the same system PostHog uses, so there's no gap in pay between the two companies for similar roles.

Four variables set your salary: your level, your step within that level, the benchmark for your role, and a location factor for where you live. The calculator uses PostHog's numbers, so you can see what that works out to:

We'd rather have a few exceptional people than a lot of good ones, and paying well is how we get them.

- If we haven't hired in your country before, we'll do the research before we make you an offer.
- If salary is the only thing stopping you from applying, our model has probably got your situation wrong. Tell us during hiring and we'll take another look.

### Level

Your level is about the impact you have. It isn't a title, and it isn't a ranking of who matters most. Roughly, the levels are:

- Junior: contributes to small or focused projects. We rarely hire here.
- Intermediate: owns projects with small to medium impact.
- Senior: owns high-impact work end to end, deciding what needs doing and getting it done.
- Staff: everything a senior does, plus regularly driving large projects that shape the whole company.

These are a guide, not exhaustive determining factors.

### Step

Each level has four steps, which give us room to recognize progress without a change of level:

- Learning: getting up to speed.
- Established: doing what the role needs.
- Thriving: going beyond it.
- Expert: going beyond it, consistently.

Most people join at established. The exceptions are people right at the start of their career or new to this kind of role. Starting there gives everyone a fair shot and leaves room for your pay to grow.

### Benchmark

Every role is benchmarked against the market rate in your area. We use Pave as our main source of salary data, and set a target range from it.

The location factor reflects what it costs to hire for your role where you live, not what it costs to live there. Every factor has a floor, so people who happen to live somewhere with a low cost of living aren't paid far less. The factor already accounts for your local exchange rate, and you're paid in your local currency unless there's a strong reason not to be.

### Relocating

If you move, your pay may be adjusted up or down for your new location at the next review. An increase needs approving in advance, because the budget won't always allow it. A few countries are also outside where we can [hire](/handbook/hiring/).

## Equity

Everyone here plays a part in the company's success, so everyone should share in it. You get significant equity and, as Gradiated doesn't have a separate cap table from PostHog, you'll receive these as PostHog share options, based on your role and level.

- Four years of vesting with a one-year cliff, starting on your first day rather than after probation
- Ten years to exercise your options if you leave
- Double-trigger acceleration, so if you're let go because of an acquisition, everything vests
- Tax-advantaged EMI or CSOP options for people in the UK, where eligible

You're also eligible for a yearly refresh worth 18% to 25% of a new grant for your current role, depending on performance. [Share options](/handbook/share-options/) has the full details.

## Probation period

We do everything we can to set you up well, but it takes time for both sides to know whether this is the right fit. Your first six months are a probation period. In that time you can leave with one month's notice, and if we end things, we pay you four weeks' salary.

Your manager keeps an eye on how it's going through your [30, 60, and 90-day check-ins](/handbook/onboarding/), and raises concerns early rather than at the end. Nobody sends a "you passed" message. By then, the check-ins will have said it.

## Severance

Even average performance gets a generous severance. If we end your contract after your first six months, you get four months of base salary, which includes any notice the law requires.

Nobody should be stuck in a role that isn't working because they can't afford to leave it. That's why this applies whenever the reason is performance or a change in what the business needs. It doesn't apply to misconduct.

## Contracts and payroll

PostHog employs everyone at Gradiated. In the UK, the US, or Germany, you're employed directly by PostHog's company there. Anywhere else, you're employed through Deel, PostHog's employer of record, or occasionally as a contractor who invoices through Deel and handles their own taxes.

- UK staff and international contractors are paid monthly, on or before the last working day.
- US staff are paid twice a month, on the 15th and the last day of the month.
- Deel pays on the last working day of the month.
