## Our approach to hiring

We're building a small team of exceptional people who are driven by ownership and hold themselves to a high bar.

At this early stage we hire through a mix of inbound and outbound. Word-of-mouth recommendations bring us some of our best people, and we also source people whose technical depth would compound with the team we already have.

The hiring process should feel like working here: open, communicative, and quick. In practice that means:

- Writing job descriptions that are specific and useful, rewriting them often
- Making our careers page and application process the best we can make them
- Giving candidates we don't hire honest, useful feedback wherever we can
- Running a smooth, fast process from application to offer

## Countries where we employ people

Most roles are based in [Cambridge or London](/handbook/in-person/), and a handful are remote. People in remote roles travel to Cambridge a couple of times a year. For remote roles, there are some limits on where we can employ people:

- You need to be physically based between GMT+2 and GMT-8. We can't hire outside that range, even if you're happy to work different hours.
  - Some roles list a narrower preferred time zone range, so the team hiring can spread its coverage and still work closely together.
- US sanctions mean we can't hire anyone in Cuba, Iran, North Korea, or Syria.

  - In some of these countries we can hire contractors instead, as long as there's no risk of the role being misclassified. We've done this successfully in Brazil and Uruguay.

## Hiring process

### External recruiters

We do all our recruiting in-house and don't work with agencies for any role. Agencies still get in touch regularly but the best response is no response. If they attach candidate profiles or CVs, don't open them. If you're ever unsure, forward the message to team@gradiated.com.

### How we write job descriptions

These are the things that work best for us:

- Be clear and precise about what the person will actually work on, linking to example PRs or issues for similar work in GitHub where we can
- Explain why this role in particular is exciting, and the impact the person will have
- Link to as much useful context as possible
- Keep requirements to the absolute minimum, with five must-haves at most
- Run the text through a [gender decoder](https://gender-decoder.katmatfield.com/) to catch unconscious bias
- Never use years of experience as a requirement

## Managing candidates

Every candidate lives in [Ashby](https://www.ashbyhq.com/), and every conversation with them is recorded there. Even candidates we don't hire should leave feeling they were treated well.

Ashby is easy to pick up, but a few tips help:

- [This getting started guide](https://docs.ashbyhq.com/getting-started-user-guides) covers almost everything you need to review candidates and leave feedback.
- If you're in direct contact with candidates, link your Gmail account in Ashby's settings. Emails you send from your inbox are then saved to the candidate's record, where the whole hiring team can see them.
- When you email a candidate from Ashby, you can pick a template from the dropdown and edit it. If you find yourself writing the same email twice, save it as a template.
- If someone applies to you by email, Slack, or anywhere else outside Ashby, and you think we should definitely interview them, ask them to apply through the [careers page](/careers/) and forward their message to team@gradiated.com. You'll get plenty of messages on LinkedIn too. Only pass the high-priority ones to <Person name="Scott Lewis" />.

## Hiring stages

### Application

We read every application properly. An application that hasn't been tailored to the role suggests you're not that excited about working here. You don't need a cover letter, but by interview stage we'll be paying attention to how much you care about what we're doing.

### Reapplying

To keep things fair for everyone, please wait 12 months before applying for the same role, or a very similar one, again. It gives you time to build up new experience, though applying again doesn't guarantee you'll get further.

### Interviews

All our interviews are in English. Most are video calls, so you'll need a working webcam. If you're based in or near Cambridge or London, one of them will probably be in person, over a coffee.

### How interviews are scored

Each interviewer scores the candidate from one to four:

1. Strong no: not right for Gradiated, now or later.
2. No: not right now, but maybe in future.
3. Yes: a solid hire.
4. Strong yes: someone exceptional we need to hire, and worth going out of our way for.

If you're torn between a two and a three, it's a two. Putting a _maybe_ through is almost never worth it, for us or for them. Add your feedback in Ashby by the end of the day after the interview, because good candidates don't wait around.

### 1. Discovery chat

The first conversation is a relaxed chat with <Person name="James Hawkins" /> about your career so far: what you're looking for, the experience you bring, and whether what we're building is what you want to work on.

### 2. Technical interview

The second is with <Person name="Josh Snyder" />, and goes deeper on the work itself: the day-to-day problems you'd take on, your technical skills, and how your approach changes as scale and complexity grow.

### 3. SuperDay

The last stage is a SuperDay: a full day of paid work with us, arranged around your schedule. Every hire does one. If you'd rather not, we'll have to part ways there.

If a whole day in one go is hard, because you can't get the time off or have childcare to plan around, we can be very flexible. We can split the SuperDay across two or more sessions, and fit it to your time zone. Nobody loses out because they can't do a SuperDay straight away.

You'll work on a task like the day-to-day work of the role. It's your chance to show what you can do, and ours to see the quality and speed of your work and how you communicate.

Everyone doing a given role gets the same task, shared at the start of the day. It's deliberately more than one person can finish in a day, so we can see how you prioritize and get things done. Spend at least 80% of your time on the task and less than 20% meeting people, because we decide based on what you produce.

For some roles the task runs over several days. For example, Engineer Journalist candidates have had three-day tasks to produce a piece of writing.

We pay a flat $1,000 for a SuperDay. Occasionally we have to cancel a scheduled SuperDay because we've filled the role with a candidate further along in the process. When that happens, we pay $500 for the time you've already put in.

If you can't accept payment, we donate it to the [Django Girls Foundation](https://djangogirls.org/en/donate/donate/) instead. Payments and donations go out on Wednesdays, so timing depends on the day of your SuperDay. If you haven't been paid within a week, let Scott know.

### Preparing for a SuperDay

A good SuperDay takes some setting up beforehand:

- Email the candidate to schedule it as early as possible, because they'll often need to book a day off work.
- SuperDay tasks never use our source code or other confidential material, so candidates don't need an NDA. If a task is close to real work for Gradiated, ask them to check their current employment contract allows it. This is why we use made-up tasks.
- US candidates need to send us a [W-9](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-9) for tax purposes, unless they're donating their payment.
- <Person name="Scott Lewis" /> has a short informal call with the candidate
  beforehand, so they know what to expect on the day and who they'll meet.
- When scheduling in Ashby, turn on the option to create a private Slack channel for the candidate and everyone involved (`#superday-firstname-role`). Invite the candidate as a single-channel guest, so they can ask questions during the day. That channel is the only access a candidate gets, so don't share anything confidential in it. Remove the guest account within one business day of the SuperDay ending, as our [access control policy](https://github.com/Gradiated/gradiated/blob/main/docs/security/policies/system-access-control-policy.md) requires.
- On the day, give the candidate a warm welcome and make clear the team is around for questions. After that, leave them to get on with the task and trust them to message if they need anything.

### Deciding

We aim to decide within 48 hours of the SuperDay. Being decisive matters here, because great candidates are usually weighing up other offers.

Everyone involved in the SuperDay leaves their feedback in Ashby as soon as they can, since the final decision leans heavily on it.

If we don't make an offer, we tell the candidate clearly why: what went well, and specific things to improve. We offer a call to talk it through, and leave the door open for them to apply again in 12 to 18 months, because people and circumstances change.

### Making the offer

Hooray!

James and Josh give the final sign-off. Scott then usually runs the offer call, sharing feedback from the process, explaining why we think the role is worth taking, covering salary, equity, and benefits as set out in [compensation](/handbook/compensation/), and answering any open questions. Whoever makes the offer follows up by email with all the details.

Once the candidate accepts, Scott starts their [onboarding](/handbook/onboarding/), lets the remaining candidates know, and closes the role.

### How Ashby works for interviewers

We pay for Ashby per seat, so to keep costs down, interviewers only see the candidates they're interviewing. For each of those, you'll see their application, including any cover letter, their CV, and all the feedback left so far.

We keep a couple of extra seats for calibration. If you'd like to see more of the pipeline and how other interviewers assess candidates, ask Scott on Slack. He can give you access for a few days or a week.

## Visa sponsorship

We hire internationally, which sometimes means sponsoring a visa. PostHog is the employer, so sponsorship follows its policy, and for now we can only sponsor visas in the UK.

- If you're already in the UK on a visa, such as a work or youth mobility visa, or need a new one to stay, such as switching from a student visa, we'll cover the cost, whether you're joining or already work here.
- If you'd need a visa to move to the UK, we can't cover the visa or relocation costs.

If PostHog has paid for your visa and you choose to leave within your first 12 months, you'll pay back a share of the cost for each month short of a year. Leave after ten months, for example, and you'd repay two-twelfths.

We can't hire anyone who needs US visa sponsorship, including H-1B sponsorship or transfers.

## E-Verify

Every new US hire goes through [E-Verify](https://www.e-verify.gov/about-e-verify), which confirms they're eligible to work in the US and lets us hire in more states. We never use it to screen candidates.

## Location and time zones

Some teams benefit from covering a wide spread of time zones. That gets us closer to round-the-clock cover for incidents, which matters most for infrastructure and pipeline work.

Teams working on something early, with no users yet, are better off within a few time zones of each other. That makes it easier to get together in person and to work synchronously.

## Internships

Students get in touch about internships _a lot_, and we're always flattered! We don't offer internships, placements, or work experience yet, because we're too scrappy to do them well.

Once you graduate, you're very welcome to apply for a full-time role on the [careers page](/careers/). Your application goes straight to our hiring team (who are real people!), and you'll hear back shortly after.

## Post-mortems

We won't get every hiring decision right. When someone leaves during their probation period, or soon after, we hold a post-mortem to work out what went wrong. It isn't about blame. We look for one or two high-impact changes to make the hiring process better.
