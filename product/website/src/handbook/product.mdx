Our [mission](/handbook/mission/) is to make open intelligence the practical choice. One long-term engineering goal is to get more useful work out of each joule.

Tokens per joule is one measure of serving efficiency. It helps us compare systems once we know they can do the same work to the same standard.

Cost is important, but it is shaped by electricity markets, hardware prices, cloud margins, subsidies, and geography. Energy efficiency is more fundamental. It tells us how much intelligence a system can produce from a finite physical resource, regardless of where it runs or who is paying for it.

It also gives every layer of our work a common direction. Advances in chips, systems, compilers, models, and inference software should all help produce more useful intelligence from each joule.

This matters because energy is a constraint on both scale and access. More efficient inference means greater capacity within a data centre, longer operation on a device, and useful intelligence in places where power and infrastructure are scarce. It opens applications that are currently too expensive, too power-hungry, or simply impossible.

Cost tells us whether intelligence is affordable today. Energy efficiency determines how abundant it can become.
