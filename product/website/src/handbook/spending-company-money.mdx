You'll often need to spend company money to do your job. We run lean, because every dollar we don't spend buys us more time to make the company work. But it matters more that you're productive, healthy, and happy, and saving money at the cost of getting work done is a false economy.

## We give context, not permission

Our expense policy runs on judgment rather than sign-off. It's similar to the trust-based approach PostHog takes, inspired by the book _No Rules Rules_.

Before you spend, ask yourself:

- Can I explain why this is in Gradiated's best interest?
- Is the cost proportionate to the benefit?
- Would I be comfortable explaining the expense to the whole company?

If both answers are yes, the expense almost certainly helps the company and your work, so go ahead. If either is no, think again.

When you're unsure, ask for context, not permission. Post in <PrivateLink href="https://gradiated.slack.com/archives/C0BSJV3492N">#work-ops</PrivateLink>, or ask James or Josh for context before spending.

## What you can spend

Each person has a $5,000 monthly User Limit in Brex. Use it for individual software, coworking or collaboration, ordinary equipment, training, and other routine expenses that help you do your job. Ask for a limit increase in Brex if a justified purchase will take you over it.

The limit is a ceiling, not an allowance or a target. Only spend what you can justify under the test above.

Use the relevant company-wide budget for company software, offsites, onboarding, and other centrally managed costs. Laptops and Apple Studio Displays are bought centrally through People & Ops rather than from your User Limit.

## Paying and documenting

Anything over $75 or £75 needs an itemized receipt attached and its memo filled in within 14 days of the charge - our auditors require it.

Upload receipts and write memos in the Brex app, or by WhatsApp, email, or Slack. For your own spending, have receipts sent to your @gradiated.com address and/or forward them to receipts@brex.com, which matches them to the charge for you. For shared or larger company expenses, send invoices and receipts to finance@gradiated.com.

The receipt has to be itemized, because Brex checks it automatically against the charge. That means the full booking confirmation for a flight or hotel, or the detailed bill for a team dinner. A cropped photo of the total or a card machine slip tells us little about what was bought, so it doesn't count.

All invoices (UK included) should be addressed to PostHog Inc. at its billing address, which you'll find in the shared 1Password vault.

A good memo covers three things:

- What: the item or service you bought
- Why: how it helps Gradiated or your work
- Context: the project, event, or people involved

In extreme cases, an expense over $75 or £75 with no receipt may be deducted from your pay if we can't confirm its business purpose. This is for repeat offenders who are clearly ignoring the policy, not for an honest slip up.

## Finance reviews the big expenses and samples the rest

PostHog's Finance team reviews:

- Every expense over $1,000
- A random sample of expenses under $1,000

Documentation matters because missing receipts and memos turn into work at audit time. Auditors chase those expenses, which means hours of back and forth with you about charges from months ago. Uploading a receipt and writing a memo now takes a minute and saves everyone that.

We're also legally barred from paying personal expenses on anyone's behalf, and we risk penalties if it happens.

<Callout type="caution" title="Why this matters">
A flexible, trust-based policy only works if everyone keeps their records up to date. If receipts and memos keep going missing, we'll have to bring in pre-approval for all spending, or treat repeated misses as a performance issue. We'd much rather do neither.
</Callout>

## Brex budgets keep the books straight

We don't police spending with hard limits, but we still use budgets and limits in Brex. They keep audits simple, and categorizing spend properly matters for board reporting, tax compliance, and tracking how we're doing against our budget.

## Contracts and recurring costs

Some spending isn't a personal subscription or a one-off purchase. It's a platform we run our work on, on a contract that bills continuously and at scale. GPU capacity, cloud compute, and model provider usage are the obvious examples for us. These can run to thousands or hundreds of thousands of dollars, so they deserve more care than a personal subscription. The same principles apply, they just carry more weight.

This section is about managing a contract we already have. Whether to adopt a new tool in the first place is a separate decision.

The person with the most context on a contract owns its usage and spend, usually whoever proposed it or runs it day to day. That includes watching usage against the limits agreed when we signed, so nobody is surprised by the bill. The easiest way to stay on top of it is to fold it into a regular review with the vendor, such as a quarterly business review, rather than looking only when something goes wrong. Keep <Person name="James Hawkins" /> or <Person name="Josh Snyder" /> in the loop early, so they can support you.

Flag an overage as soon as it looks likely to persist or to make a real difference to the bill. Don't wait for it to land a few times first: the point of watching usage is to catch it before the money is spent.

- The person with the most context drives it, and keeps James or Josh aware. They decide whether the extra spend is worth it or whether we scale back.
- Email finance@gradiated.com as soon as the overage looks material, so Finance can forecast it.
- Bring in legal for any new contract, or when the terms change. A straight renewal on the same terms usually doesn't need it.
- Finance@gradiated.com is the billing email for every vendor contract.

## Answers to common questions

Can I pay for a work expense with my personal card?

No. Every work expense goes on Brex. The company earns points on card spend, and Finance doesn't have time to process lots of reimbursements.

What if I paid for a work expense with my personal card by mistake?

Submit a reimbursement in Brex within 90 days, with the itemized receipt and a memo explaining what it was for and why you didn't use Brex. We can't reimburse anything without a receipt.

Can I use my Brex card for something personal?

No.

What if I charged the wrong limit?

Reassign the charge to the right limit in Brex.

Can I upload lots of receipts at once?

Yes. Brex lets you attach receipts to several expenses in one go.

What billing email should I give a vendor?

finance@gradiated.com, whether it's for paying bills or signing a contract.

A vendor contract is running into overages or about to cost more. What should I do?

Flag it early. Whoever knows the contract best drives it, keeps James or Josh aware, and emails finance@gradiated.com once the overage looks material. You decide what counts as material. The section on contracts above has the detail.

What if I drive for work?

Claim mileage through Brex. Don't expense fuel separately.

What if I put a personal expense on my Brex card by mistake?

Find the charge in Brex and select Repay. In the US and Canada, Brex pulls the money from your linked bank account automatically. Everywhere else, Repay only records the expense, and you transfer the amount from your personal account to the company's. Ask Scott for the bank details.

How do I get into WeWork?

We have an All Access account. Ask Scott to add you.

I've received a bill that Finance needs to pay. What do I do?

Check it, then submit it through Bill Pay in Brex, with what it's for and which budget it comes from. Bills are paid when PostHog's Finance team runs its weekly bill run, on Wednesdays.

Can I upgrade a flight with my own points or money?

Yes. Book the economy fare on Brex, then upgrade it yourself. If premium travel can only be booked with your own points or card, claim back the economy fare in Brex, with a screenshot of the economy price when you booked.

## Personal spending isn't a company expense

Auditors flag anything that looks personal as a non-business expense, because it counts as a taxable benefit we've given you.

That includes:

- Personal costs on a work trip, like a gym session, a fitness class, or groceries
- Subscriptions for your own entertainment, like Spotify, Netflix, or Amazon Prime
- Anything that mainly benefits you rather than the company
- Anything you wouldn't be comfortable explaining to the whole team

If an expense has both a work and a personal benefit, charge the company only for the reasonable work share.

If you genuinely thought something was in the company's interest and we disagree, we'll talk it through and explain why. If you knowingly spend company money for your own benefit, or deliberately work around these guidelines, we'll probably treat it as serious misconduct.

## Defaults for common expenses

### Equipment

PostHog People & Ops buy every MacBook. Buying them centrally keeps our accounting and fixed asset records accurate for the audit. Laptops stay company property, and you send yours back when you leave.

- We don't buy Apple Studio Displays. Buy a monitor from your User Limit instead: the Alogic Clarity Pro 27" and the LG UltraFine 27" are both good, at a fraction of the price.
- Engineers get a 14-inch MacBook Pro with an M5 Pro chip (18-core CPU and 20-core GPU), 64GB of RAM, and a 1TB SSD.
- Sales and customer roles get a 14-inch MacBook Pro with an M5 chip, the 10-core GPU, and the 32GB RAM upgrade.
- Everyone else gets a MacBook Pro. Where we can, we pass on engineering machines that are no longer in use, so you get the extra power.
- We only buy laptops with an English keyboard (US, international, or British), so they're easy to pass on when you upgrade or leave.
- If you ever need to buy a laptop yourself, check Amazon for a sale before buying from Apple, use your Brex card, and skip AppleCare, which isn't good value.
- Ask Scott for a new laptop if yours is over four years old, is an engineering machine with less than 48GB of RAM, or is really slowing you down. Check first that it isn't a setup problem, like another app eating your memory.
- Phones and iPads are only for people whose role depends on them. Ask Scott if that's you.

Keyboards, mice, and laptop stands go on your User Limit. Look for discounts, and buy refurbished where it makes sense: it usually works just as well. The same goes for a desk and chair. If you work from home some days, expense the monitor, keyboard, and other kit you need to work properly there.

### Software

We're strongly against adding new collaboration software. The upside has to be very large to be worth it, because every new shared tool is another place for to-dos, comments, and conversations to live. That adds far more complexity than it looks.

Individual software is your call, and we'd love you to share anything good you find. If a tool you use on your own catches on across the team, we'll move it to a company account with Scott as admin. To use a company tool, sign in with your @gradiated.com account or request access in the tool itself. There's no separate request process.

AI coding tools, like Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex, are encouraged, but usage-based pricing can climb quickly. Most engineers spend about the cost of one max-tier subscription, roughly $200 a month. If yours is running several times higher, that's usually a misconfiguration or an inefficient workflow rather than a real need. Compare setups with your teammates, and ask in #work-ops if you're not sure.

IDEs vary a lot in price. The top suites can cost $700, which isn't good value for most engineers.

### Coworking

We have a WeWork All Access account, so use it wherever there's a WeWork near you. Ask Scott to add you. If you work remotely and there's no WeWork nearby, you can pay for a coworking membership from your User Limit.

### Working in Cambridge

We work in person in Cambridge, usually two or three days a week, so a few everyday costs come up often.

- Commuting: don't expense your train fare or other travel to and from the office. HMRC treats commuting the company pays for as a taxable benefit, so it would be taxed much like salary and add reporting work for Finance. Instead, we take commuting costs into account when we set your salary.
- Lunch: pay for everyday lunches yourself, including lunch with colleagues at the office. Meals on work trips and meals with customers or candidates are company expenses.
- Coffee: coffee for a meeting is a company expense. Coffee just for yourself isn't.
- Hotels: an occasional night in Cambridge is fine, for example between back-to-back office days or after a late evening. One or two nights every week adds up quickly, and we don't cover that by default. Talk to James or Josh if your week needs it.

### Travel

- We fly economy by default and don't pay for business class.
- If your plans might change, it can be worth paying a little more for a flexible fare that refunds in full to your Brex card.
- An occasional upgrade to premium economy is fine if you travel a lot for work, the price isn't unreasonable, and you're working the next day.
- For flights over $1,000, take a refund to your Brex card rather than travel credit if you cancel.
- If you travel regularly to a country that offers it, consider a trusted traveler program like Global Entry, paid for on Brex. It saves a lot of time, especially entering the US.
- Abroad, expense a reasonably priced eSIM. We don't cover roaming charges.
- Pay in the local currency when you use Brex abroad, since Brex usually gets a better exchange rate.
- Everyone at Gradiated is employed by PostHog, so you're covered by PostHog's international travel insurance on work trips. Don't buy your own.
- You can fly out or back on a different day from the one you're needed, as long as the fare is about the same or cheaper. We don't cover any costs outside the days you need to be there.
- When you visit customers or prospects, look for a chance to share a meal. It doesn't need to be extravagant, just appropriate to the customer. If you'd be happy to justify it to the whole team, you're probably fine. Use your User Limit and request an increase in Brex if you need it.

If you're asked to travel at short notice for something urgent, we may pay for a better seat, especially on a long flight. Ask Scott if you think that applies to you. This is for genuine one-offs only.

### Trips to Cambridge

If you work remotely, coming to Cambridge is the best way to work closely with the rest of the team. There's no separate travel budget: pay for these trips from your User Limit, and we'd encourage you to.

Make each trip count. Line it up with something specific, like a project you'll work on together in person or a few colleagues being in the office the same week. Coming in to sit with your headphones on doesn't make the most of the trip.
