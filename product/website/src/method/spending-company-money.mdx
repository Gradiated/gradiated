Company money should remove constraints that matter to the work. Spend it carefully, but do not let a small, sensible expense slow you down.

## Use judgement

For routine, reversible purchases, you do not need permission. Before spending, be able to answer three questions:

- How does this help Gradiated?
- Is the cost proportionate to the benefit?
- Would I be comfortable explaining it to the whole company?

If the answers are clear, make the purchase. A budget or card limit is a ceiling, not a target.

## Get context first

Talk to the person responsible for the area before committing us to something expensive, recurring, difficult to unwind, or shared across the company. Do the same when a purchase introduces a vendor, handles company or customer data, or is likely to grow materially with usage.

This is not an approval ritual. The aim is to expose trade-offs while they are still cheap to change and to make sure somebody owns the cost over time.

## Make every expense legible

Use the company payment method where possible. Add the itemized receipt and a short note promptly: what you bought, why it was useful, and which project or people it was for. Good records save time, make tax and audit work possible, and let us keep the policy based on trust.

Never put a personal expense through the company. If an expense mixes work and personal benefit, charge the company only for the reasonable business portion. If you make a mistake, say so and correct it quickly.

## Prefer the practical option

Buy what makes the work meaningfully better, not simply the cheapest option and not the luxurious one. Reuse an existing tool before adding another place for work to live. Travel economy by default. Choose equipment for productive use and a sensible working life.

When the better option costs more, explain the reason in the expense note. The test is not whether something is inexpensive; it is whether the company gets enough value from it.
