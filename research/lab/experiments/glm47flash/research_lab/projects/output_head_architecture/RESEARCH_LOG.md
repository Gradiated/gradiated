# Research log

## 2026-07-15 — Exact head spectrum and cost falsifier

Question: does the exact untied BF16 `lm_head.weight` support a factorization or shortlist with a sustainable >=10% c16 cost path?

Action: bound `lm_head.weight` to the local BF16 checkpoint index (`75fce703`, shard `model-00047-of-00048.safetensors`), read all 154,880 x 2,048 BF16 values by mmap, accumulate the exact 2,048 x 2,048 Gram matrix, and eigendecompose it. Also measured row-norm concentration as a cheap shortlist proxy. Reproducible code and raw result are [`head_spectrum_proxy.py`](head_spectrum_proxy.py) and [`HEAD_SPECTRUM_PROXY.json`](HEAD_SPECTRUM_PROXY.json); decision artifact is [`DECISION_2026-07-15.json`](DECISION_2026-07-15.json). No provider mutation or paid GPU run was used.

Evidence: the head is 634,388,480 BF16 bytes. Singular energy is diffuse: rank 512 captures 61.32% Frobenius energy (relative error 0.622), rank 768 captures 71.97% (error 0.529), and 99% requires rank 1,804. At rank 1,804, factor weights and ideal matmul work are about 88.1% of full; applying that saving to the retained 14.09% vocabulary-projection interval ceiling yields only about 1.67% optimistic endpoint-cost reduction. Ranks 512 and 768 could clear the 10% arithmetic ceiling only by accepting severe semantics-changing proxy error (about 10.5% and 8.8% optimistic endpoint reductions respectively). Norm-based shortlists are not supported: top 1/5/10% rows contain only 3.21/8.63/14.85% of row-norm energy, and no token-usage or quality evidence makes those rows meaningful candidates.

Chip fit: a full single-token projection is 634,388,480 FLOPs and 634 MB of BF16 weight traffic before cache effects; at the measured 535 tok/s this is a 339 GB/s raw-traffic scale, not a demonstrated wall-time requirement. L40S therefore has a possible bandwidth angle, but H100 and MI355X have substantially richer bandwidth and do not make this head a stronger cheaper-chip lever. A factorized implementation would also replace one supported large GEMM with two smaller GEMMs and extra intermediate traffic; the ideal ratios above are arithmetic/storage bounds, not kernel or serving measurements.

Interpretation: factorization does not show a recoverable modest-rank path, and shortlist evidence is insufficient. The 14.09% trace figure is itself an optimistic component-time ceiling, not a wall-time share, so the calculated endpoint reductions are upper bounds rather than serving results. The lane does not support the campaign's $0.571/M to <=$0.514/M promotion requirement.

Decision/next move: close this lane with no paid endpoint experiment. Return capacity to the remaining MoE/depth/MLA-rank/draft-head portfolio. Reopen only if token-usage traces or post-training experiments demonstrate recovery at rank <=512 (or a usage-grounded shortlist), which this turn did not establish.

Correction after chip-level review: the 339 GB/s figure above is a no-cross-row-reuse upper bound, not the expected c16 stream. The exact c16 GEMM is `[16,2048] x [2048,154880]`; if each weight tile is read once per decode step and reused across 16 rows, 535.286 output tok/s implies about 33.46 steps/s and a 21.2 GB/s lower-bound weight stream. This strengthens the decision not to infer a DRAM bottleneck from the projection's duration. Small-M occupancy, launch geometry, cache behavior, and actual bytes must be measured before reopening the lane.
